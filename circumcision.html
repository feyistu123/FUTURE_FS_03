Male Circumcision

A Safe, Simple and Effective Procedure

Overview

Circumcision is the surgical removal of the foreskin (prepuce) covering the glans penis. It is performed for medical, cultural, religious or personal health reasons.

Procedure Time: 15-30 minutes
Recovery: 7-10 days
Performed by: Trained Healthcare Professionals
Safe for all ages

Benefits of Circumcision

Reduced UTI Risk

Reduces risk of urinary tract infections, especially in infants.

Prevents Phimosis

Prevents phimosis (tight foreskin) and paraphimosis.

STI Prevention

Reduces risk of HIV and other sexually transmitted infections.

Lower Cancer Risk

Low risk of penile cancer and easier hygiene maintenance.

Types of Circumcision

Dorsal Slit Method

Foreskin is crushed at 12 o'clock position, a dorsal slit is made, and foreskin is excised. Edges are sutured or clamped.

Sleeve Resection Method

Foreskin is separated from glans, measured, and cut. The excess foreskin is removed and edges are sutured.

Plastibell Method

A plastic ring (Plastibell) is placed over the glans, foreskin is tied over the ring, and it falls off naturally in 5-10 days. Common in children.

Procedure Steps

1

Position the Patient

Patient is positioned supine and properly secured.

2

Clean the Penis

The area is cleaned with antiseptic solution.

3

Administer Local Anesthesia

Dorsal penile block or ring block is administered.

4

Separate Foreskin

Foreskin is gently separated from glans using a probe or artery forceps.

5

Incision & Excision

Foreskin is incised and excised using the appropriate technique.

6

Hemostasis

Control any bleeding points.

7

Apply Dressing

Antibiotic ointment and sterile gauze are applied.

8

Aftercare Instructions

Caregiver receives detailed aftercare instructions.

Aftercare Instructions

Keep Clean & Dry

Keep the area clean and dry. Change dressing daily or if soiled.

Apply Ointment

Apply antiseptic/antibiotic ointment as advised by your doctor.

Pain Management

Take prescribed pain relief (e.g., Paracetamol) as needed.

Avoid Tight Clothing

Avoid tight diapers or clothing that may irritate the area.

Possible Complications

Bleeding
Infection
Pain & Swelling
Poor Wound Healing
Meatal Stenosis (rare)
Excessive or Inadequate Removal

When to Seek Medical Care

Persistent bleeding
Fever or pus discharge
Severe pain or swelling
Plastibell not fallen off after 14 days
Difficulty in urination
